Flying safely and legally

Drones in New Zealand are covered by the Civil Aviation Authority’s rules, and some places in Auckland have their own rules too. Here’s how every Birds-Eyes job is planned around them.

  • CAA Part 101

    Every flight follows Civil Aviation Rule Part 101, the rules for small drones in New Zealand. Both drones in the fleet are small and light, and they’re flown within the limits Part 101 sets.

  • Airspace checked for every address

    Much of Auckland sits near Auckland Airport, Whenuapai, Ardmore, North Shore Aerodrome or a helipad. Before a shoot is confirmed, the airspace over your address is checked. If clearance is needed, it’s requested through AirShare first. If it isn’t given, the shoot doesn’t go ahead.

  • Permission first

    The drone only flies over your property with your permission. It doesn’t fly over neighbouring houses or people who haven’t agreed, and shots are framed to keep the focus on your place.

  • Always in sight, only in good weather

    The drone stays in view the whole flight and only flies in daylight. If rain or strong wind is forecast, the shoot moves to another day.

  • Parks and public places

    Auckland Council has its own rules for parks. Some places, including Tūpuna Maunga (Auckland’s volcanic cones), cemeteries and the Botanic Gardens, are never flown over, and commercial filming in parks needs a permit.

  • Your privacy

    Photos and video are of your property, for you. Nothing is shown in the gallery or online without your permission. Read the privacy policy for how your details are handled.
